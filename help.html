Buying

What does it cost to bid?

Nothing to browse. Verification is a one-time £5. Entering an auction places a £50 hold on your card — released in full after Saturday if you don't win, credited toward your payment if you do. Winners pay the hammer price plus a fee based on the sale price (see the fee table below) plus the card processing cost.

The bidding rules

Minimum first bid £150. Increments: £50 up to £999 · £100 from £1,000 to £4,999 · £200 from £5,000. Featured lots hammer on a 30-second timer, all others 15 seconds — and every bid resets the timer in full. All bids are legally binding: you tick two boxes when you enter each auction confirming you understand that.

What happens if I win but don't pay?

You have 72 hours. Miss it and the £50 hold is captured as a late fee (£25 to the seller, £25 to us) — and the bid is still binding. Walking away entirely costs a further £100 (£40 to the seller, £60 to us). Total cost of not honouring a winning bid: £150.

What if the top bid doesn't meet the reserve?

The top bid goes to the seller, who can accept it, reject it, or counter. You can counter back — numbers only, and your number can only go up. Three rounds maximum, 24 hours to respond each time; silence counts as rejection. If you agree a price, the sale completes at it.

Selling

What does it cost to sell?

The same one-time £5 ID verification as buyers if you’ve never verified (one account covers both), then £25 to list, paid before review — it covers a real person checking your listing, so it's not refundable. Optional £10 for a featured slot (21 per auction, first paid first placed): featured cars run first on Saturday, hammer on a 30-second timer and rotate on the home page. When you're paid out, the buyer's auction fee has already covered our cut — the vehicle price is yours.

The damage declaration — why it matters

Your listing includes a required box: either "no damage except normal wear and tear" or an honest list of every fault. This box is what any dispute is judged against. Everything you declare there is protected; anything you leave out can cost you the sale, and the £25 fee, if a buyer disputes.

When do I get paid?

After the buyer pays, we hold the money and share contact details both ways. You hand over the car and the New Keeper Slip, post the V5C to the DVLA, and upload proof of posting; the buyer uploads the keeper slip and two short videos of the car. Once our team checks everything matches, the money is released to your bank.

The collection clock

Once contact details are shared, agree a collection time and date with the buyer within 72 hours (the buyer confirms it in the app). After that, £5/day accrues against your payout — nothing is taken until the case resolves — capped at £70 at 14 days. A genuine, unforeseeable reason (an emergency, hospital) wipes it entirely: an admin decides. If 14 days pass without a valid reason, the sale falls, the buyer is refunded in full, and you pay the £70 plus the card processing cost of the refund.

Relisting

£5 if a reserve negotiation didn't work out. Free if your buyer defaulted or a valid emergency collapsed the sale. Full £25 after an upheld dispute — and the damage must be declared this time.

Disputes & problems

What can be disputed — and what can't

Disputable: damage or missing parts that existed when the car was listed and weren't in the seller's damage declaration. Not disputable: anything that develops after you collect — a clutch that slips three weeks later is the ordinary risk of buying at auction. That's why disputes can only be raised before you confirm receipt of the car.

How a dispute runs

You file it in the app with a description, photos and video — all payment clocks and payouts freeze immediately. An admin takes the case, emails the seller for their side, and speaks to you both on recorded calls. The decision is made in writing. Upheld: full refund, you don't keep the car, and the seller loses their listing fee. Rejected: the deal stands — complete the purchase, or exit for £100 (which goes to the seller).

Report a problem vs. a dispute

Report a problem is for transaction issues that aren't about the car's condition — mainly a seller who won't arrange collection. Attach screenshots; the seller is notified, an admin investigates. A dispute is specifically "the car is not as described."

Titles

Clean, Cat N and Cat S

Clean: no recorded write-off. Cat N: previously written off for non-structural (often cosmetic or electrical) damage, since repaired. Cat S: structural damage, since repaired — or sold as a repair project, clearly described. Every lot shows its category on the card and the listing.
